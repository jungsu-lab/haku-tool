# Perfume experience-before-product v004 critique

## Research verdict

`partial_pass`

## What the source proves

- It is one licensed, unedited 18.28-second take.
- The bottle is actually sprayed at the wrist; use is not implied with unrelated
  lifestyle footage.
- The hands later recompose and raise the same bottle in the same light and
  spatial world.

## Plain versus proof

- Both variants contain 200 frames at 25 fps and use the same five source
  anchors.
- Plain opens on the product-like hold and then visibly resets to the earlier
  spray action.
- Proof follows spray contact, spray release, wrist response, product entrance,
  and product recovery in chronological causal order.
- This changes the sequence from object-first display into ritual-first proof of
  use without hiding the product during the experience.

## Revision findings

- v001 ended after another hand had begun to obscure the bottle.
- v002 fixed the timing but the 16:9 frame left the object too small.
- v003 used a fixed 9:16 crop and made the in-frame hand-product event legible,
  but its final source range still ran into occlusion.
- v004 uses one contiguous 10.0-12.0 second recovery and ends while the bottle
  remains visible.

## Boundary findings

- The first cut preserves the spray gesture and exposes a real mist event.
- The second cut advances from spray to wrist response rather than resetting the
  action.
- The product begins to rise naturally after the wrist state.
- The final 25+25 frame recovery is spatially and chronologically continuous.
- No zoom, freeze, reverse, retime, animated crop, or last-frame padding is used.

## Why it is not a full pass

The bottle has no readable label, claim, or isolated brand packshot. The edit
validates the reusable experience-before-object sequence grammar, but it cannot
prove a complete advertising recovery or product identity.
