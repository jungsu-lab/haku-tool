## What passes

The source contains one real, stable blue–white–orange–red refracted band in a locked setup. The edit transfers that same physical carrier from empty wall to fingertips, palm, turning hand, two-hand interception, and open-palm release. Each state changes how the body receives the light without inventing a new optical event.

V2 removes the near-camera enlargement and dark silhouette that invalidated V1. The last transition now retains comparable hand scale, band height, and carrier legibility. No generated glow, exposure pumping, zoom, freeze, retime, reverse, animated crop, transition, or padding is used.

## Why the plain comparison matters

Plain and proof use the same source events and total duration. The proof's chronological carrier order makes the optical event develop from environment to body, while the comparison lacks that deliberate relay. The result is evidence for an in-shot luminous relay, not merely a shared palette.

## Learned gate

Name both the optical phenomenon and its carrier at every state. A transition passes only while the destination preserves the carrier's readability; reject near-camera occlusion, unrelated silhouette dominance, or body-scale lurch even when the source light is real.

## Scope limit

This validates one locked single-take rainbow/refraction wall-to-skin branch. Water reflections, moving cameras, night light, mixed locations, mixed orientation, and music remain untested. It is not a user acceptance.
