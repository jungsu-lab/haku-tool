## Cross-work findings

### Timeline grammar layer

- Store each meaningful edit as an exact time-range event with ten required axes: narrative, shot, cut, timing, transition, text, color, audio, motion, and purpose.
- Purpose is not an effect label. Record what the effect is, when it occurs, which scene receives it, why it is used, and what the viewer is expected to feel.
- Keep work-level semantic review and event-level grammar separate. The first explains the whole sequence; the second makes the exact editing decision reusable and testable.
- Link each event to zero or more Operators only after direct review. A single attractive push-in, black frame, or close-up is not an Operator until the same cause-and-effect structure repeats across works and survives proof cycles.
- When audio is deferred, do not infer bass hits, beat positions, SFX, or sound anchors from visual cuts or automatic onset estimates.
- `DVdX-WBk4L5` proves that action and relationship grammar can be stored at shot resolution: location master → shared prop setup → causal material detail → scale breath → new hand action → body bridge → tactile peak → black chapter breath → pair reentry → feet onset → full-body payoff → black breath → seated resolution → intimate final image.
- `DQi8EW5k0-o` provides the opposite density case: one long luminous inhale, a 0.1667-second fixed-pulse image burst, and one quiet place-name signoff. The central device is duration contrast and image selection, not pan, zoom, shake, or transition decoration.
- Treat `breath → burst → signoff` as a grammar candidate, not a promoted Operator, until its cause-and-effect structure repeats in at least three independently reviewed works and survives a proof cycle.

### Universal core

Use these before style devices:

1. Build the edit around real action inside shots.
2. Preserve a readable anchor before or after dense clusters.
3. Connect detail, person, relationship, and environment by new information rather than crop size alone.
4. Let repeated motifs change state or meaning.
5. Use blocking and presence/absence to express relationships.
6. End on an earned person, relationship, object, light, or empty-space afterimage.

### Confirmed conditional families

- `functional-memory-panels`: each panel must have a different information role; use one dominant moving panel; when an action is the cause, reveal supporting tactile/person panels progressively rather than all at once; release to a single hero image.
- `meaningful-copy-gate`: copy must be readable and perform chapter, proposition, or emotional punctuation; never imitate tiny red copy as a decorative line.
- `luminous-texture-relay`: use real glass, water, flare, reflection, or backlight; do not create exposure pumping.
- `presence-to-absence-hold`: establish the subject or action before leaving the same environment or material empty.
- `experience-before-product`: prove use, body, relationship, or season before claim and product recovery.
- `journey-function-chain`: organize travel material as preparation → movement → dwelling → discovery → recovery, not by random location.

## Important counterexamples

- A long low-motion shot is not automatically a freeze error.
- Black frames may contain readable copy, chapter breath, or panel release.
- A thin red-looking mark in a contact sheet may be tiny typography. Inspect densely before classifying it.
- Persistent center marks can be watermarks, not style.
- Sideways pixels without rotation metadata are not experimental composition. Compare clockwise and counterclockwise normalization using horizon and gravity cues.
- Microcuts are a local density tool, not the Haku default.
- Small images are not sufficient for in-frame editing. They need role, hierarchy, common motif, and a later full-frame release.
- Automatic digital zoom must never be used to conceal weak coverage or long holds.

## Material routing

Choose by available coverage:

- One complete action in several distances: `action-as-edit-spine`
- Detail + person + environment linked by one motif: `sensory-scale-relay`
- Same person or pair in changing positions: `relationship-through-blocking`
- Subject followed by the same place or material empty: `presence-to-absence-hold`
- Environment/person/detail panels plus a hero shot: `functional-memory-panels`
- Motif continuity is not session continuity. Shared light, wind, water, or birds do not erase visible differences in lens, geography, color temperature, or capture style. Keep fit conditional and request a same-location release or real bridge instead of hiding the mismatch with effects.
- Long-take state compression: cut during motion only if the next state preserves direction; otherwise use a pose match or completed-action boundary. Never reset an object backward just because a later still looks attractive. Give consequence and after-state more time than setup when they carry the emotional destination.
- Presence-to-absence needs a visible inherited trace: the same place or surface plus residual smoke, water, fabric, dust, light, object displacement, or another readable consequence. A tiny source in near-pure black may be technically continuous but still fail as an afterimage. Do not manufacture the missing trace with unrelated smoke, exposure pumping, zoom, or freeze.
- Screen the complete source in chronological order before assigning `presence-to-absence-hold`. A title or search tag such as “walking away” is not evidence of an exit. Reject the source for this Operator if the subject remains visible in the final state, even when the motion and background are attractive.
- For a walking exit, motion direction alone is not a continuity pass. Inspect every boundary at source frame rate and reject a cut when the subject jumps too far in screen position or step phase. Save time from setup or redundant travel, not from the final approach to the edge; give the saved frames to the native empty-space consequence.
- Preserve the source aspect ratio when the event uses both horizontal edges. A vertical cover crop that deletes the entrance or exit invalidates the Operator; use vertical coverage or an explicit non-cover layout instead of animated reframing.
- For `motif-state-return`, a chronological jump may be large only when the spatial anchor remains stable and the motif advances monotonically. Inspect that shape, fill, light, object arrangement, or relationship state never resets backward. Give the completed or meaning-changed return more time than the early setup states.
- Reject already edited stock montages as proof payloads even when their shot sequence is attractive. Otherwise the source editor's decisions are falsely credited to the Operator.
- A stock file can contain a hidden retake splice even when its page describes one action. Screen object state frame by frame: if a full cup becomes empty, a used surface becomes pristine, or hand/object geometry resets without a visible causal action, reject it before proof rendering.
- For `motif-state-return`, continuity alone is not enough. The anchor and its before/after state must remain visible; reject an otherwise attractive take when hair, hands, shallow focus, framing, or an object hides the changed return.
- For `meaningful-copy-gate`, hold timing, source ranges, crop, grade, and motion constant in the plain/proof comparison. The copy passes only when removing it changes a chapter, question, proposition, or emotional punctuation.
- Copy must name or question something supported by the visible sequence. Do not invent biography, product performance, or causality that the footage cannot prove.
- Reject copy that survives only as a tiny line, sticker box, repeated template, or unreadable decoration. Readability is necessary but not sufficient; purpose and evidence must also be explicit.
- Treat copy semantics and copy styling as separate gates. Even evidence-bound text fails when all-caps weight, shadow, box, color, or scale becomes louder than the action that proves it.
- Time each clause to a visible state. A line about darkness, absence, arrival, contact, or consequence must not appear before that state is on screen. Prefer restrained regular-weight type and minimal shadow when the footage itself is quiet.
- For `experience-before-product`, product visibility during real use is allowed; the gate delays explicit object, claim, packshot, or brand emphasis rather than pretending the product is absent.
- Preserve causal order from use to bodily or material response to product recovery. An object-first opening that resets to an earlier action is not equivalent evidence, even when every shot comes from one take.
- A fixed reel crop may strengthen the hand-product relationship only when the complete required action remains inside the frame. Never animate the crop to chase missing coverage.
- Real use plus an unlabeled object recovery is only a partial advertising pass. A full pass still requires an identifiable product, claim, label, or brand recovery supported by the source.
- For `luminous-texture-relay`, name the physical phenomenon and its carrier at every step. A valid same-session chain can move real flower shadow from fabric to skin, turn skin into flower contact, and resolve as the same foreground flower crossing a face.
- Shared palette or generic sunlight is not a relay. Every cut must hand off a visible property such as shadow shape, reflection path, flare direction, translucent edge, or foreground occlusion.
- A glass prop or a stock-page “reflection/refraction” label is not optical evidence. Reject a source when only the object or its ordinary silhouette is visible and no refraction path, colored dispersion, caustic, reflection movement, translucent edge, or carrier transfer can be named on screen.
- Keep branch confidence separate. A pass for direct sunlight and flower shadow does not validate water reflection, glass refraction, lens flare, night light, or bridges between different locations.
- A luminous relay may develop inside one continuous locked setup rather than only across separate shots. Name the stable band or reflection layers, then name each carrier transition—for example wall, fingertips, palm, and open hand—so the edit is judged by visible transfer rather than shared color.
- The optical carrier must remain readable at its destination. Reject a cut when near-camera body enlargement, dark silhouette, or unrelated occlusion becomes stronger than the band, caustic, reflection, flare, or translucent edge being relayed.
- Probe every requested source range before rendering. Reject a recipe when `source_in + required_output_duration` exceeds the decoded source duration; never discover or conceal a source overflow through failed rendering, repeated frames, padding, freeze, or retime.
- For `sensory-scale-relay`, each distance must add a different information role. A useful chain can move from tactile object detail to an identifiable action owner, then an embodied reaction, and finally the full relationship or environment; four attractive crop sizes of the same fact do not pass.
- Let the required destination coverage determine the proof canvas. If a vertical cover crop deletes participants, entrance or exit, or the spatial relationship that completes the Operator, use native matching coverage or a deliberately integrated non-cover design. Do not call information-preserving but visually tiny letterboxing a finished aesthetic solution.
- Shared color alone does not bridge scale. Preserve a concrete carrier—such as the same drink, food, hands, light, location, or social action—while each cut contributes new information.
- For `relationship-through-blocking`, order states so distance, touch, action ownership, and shared direction develop monotonically. An attractive embrace placed before initial contact, or a separation inserted after shared travel, resets the relationship even when every shot contains the same pair.
- Preserve the physical hinge that changes relationship ownership. When reconvergence becomes a lift, carry, escape, handoff, or embrace, do not skip the onset merely to shorten the video. Compress redundant setup; keep the causally decisive body transition near-contiguous.
- Apply the same physical-hinge rule to object rituals. A matched stream, limb, or motion direction does not excuse deleting the pot, hand, tool, or object that owns the action. Compress a genuinely inactive hold only after the action has visibly completed.
- An empty-space ending begins only after the final body edge clears frame. A close performer crossing is still presence, not absence, and must not be mislabeled by a coarse contact sheet.
- For `functional-memory-panels`, name each panel's non-substitutable role before layout: shared presence, tactile exchange, place, action owner, product detail, or another observable function. If two panels merely show the same relationship at similar scale, remove one.
- Progressive panel entry needs a later density release. Return to one stronger live image that restores body, action, product, or spatial information the panel field intentionally withheld; otherwise the composite remains a static template.
- Describe panel evidence conservatively. A shoulder-close moving two-shot may prove shared presence but not full walking-body blocking, even when background motion implies travel.
- For `experience-before-product`, the same creator and consecutive stock IDs do not prove product or session continuity. Compare container geometry, label, color, props, surface, lighting, and action tool directly; reject the sequence if any advertised identity changes.
- Separate category recovery from brand recovery. Matching container, cream, props, and light can support an unlabeled category film, but a full brand-advertising pass still requires a readable product name, label, packshot, or source-supported claim.
- Separate product presence during use, explicit product emphasis, and brand recovery. A label can remain visible while real use and bodily response lead the edit, but that proves only the ordering and recovery branches—not a hidden-product reveal.
- A readable branded hold is not automatically a strong finale. Compare its expression, light, object scale, and movement energy against the sensory peak; if the recovery is visually flatter, keep the Operator at partial or obtain a better hero shot.
- Match the recovery's light world to the experience that earns it. When the brand hold is passive, keep only the minimum recognition duration; a longer hold can make the edit feel less premium even though the label remains clear.
- Do not trust a stock-page action description without reviewing the entire source. A page labeled as eating may contain only lid opening and smelling; name only the action that is actually visible.
- Split `experience-before-product` into three routing branches rather than pretending every Haku advertisement hides the product until the end: strict experience-before-explicit-brand, early product-in-life with delayed brand emphasis, and product-in-life followed by larger world expansion.
- Record three separate thresholds: first product visibility, first readable label, and first independent logo/claim. Product presence in a lived scene is not automatically product emphasis.
- `DYrhH-6zH-1` contains a readable Pocari bottle around 3.885–4.533 seconds and a logo checkpoint around 13.598–15.217 seconds; it cannot support the old claim that 0–45.583 seconds are product-free experience.
- `DJrTFPaTXHI` places product-in-life shots around 11.174–16.761 seconds, then long action and social/world expansion, and ends on experience rather than a product recovery. Route it to `product-in-life-world-expansion`, not strict late recovery.
- Actual copy with a defined semantic role: `meaningful-copy-gate`
- Real reflection, water, flare, or backlight coverage: `luminous-texture-relay`
- Product-in-use plus claim and hero recovery: `experience-before-product`

If the required coverage is absent, choose a smaller operator, use a simple observational edit, or request reshooting. Do not synthesize coverage with zoom, freeze, repeated frames, unrelated overlays, or reference media.
